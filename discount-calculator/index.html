FAQ

How does a sequential double discount (stacked coupon) work?

A double discount applies the second discount to the already reduced price, not the original price. For example, a $100 item with 40% off becomes $60; an extra 20% off takes $12 off the $60, resulting in a final price of $48 (a 52% total savings, not 60%).

How do I calculate the final price including sales tax?

Sales tax is calculated on the discounted sale price, not the original sticker price. Our calculator automatically applies your local tax rate to the final sale amount.

What is the formula for calculating a percentage discount?

Discount Amount = Original Price × (Discount % ÷ 100). Final Price = Original Price − Discount Amount.

Can this calculator help compare competing store sales?

Yes. You can quickly test different store deals (such as 30% off vs $15 flat coupon) to see which retailer offers the lowest final price.

Is my financial calculation data stored or shared?

No. All calculations run locally in your web browser memory. None of your pricing numbers are ever recorded or transmitted.

Understanding Retail Discounts, Sales Tax, and Double Discounts

During major shopping seasons like Black Friday, clearance events, or seasonal sales, retail price tags can be deliberately confusing. Signs advertising 'Take an extra 25% off already discounted 40% items' do not mean you get a 65% discount! Retailers apply discounts sequentially, making mental math tricky.

Our Discount Calculator helps you compute exact sale prices, total dollars saved, and final costs including state or local sales tax. Understanding whether a 'Buy 2 Get 1 Free' deal is better than a flat 30% discount empowers you to make smart spending decisions.

Simply enter the original price tag, the primary discount percentage, and any secondary coupon codes or sales tax rates. You get an immediate, itemized breakdown showing what you pay at the register and how much money stays in your wallet.
