How to Create Winning Price Quotations and Project Estimates

Before starting any commercial project or supplying goods, providing a formal price quotation sets clear expectations and builds client trust. A well-structured quotation prevents 'scope creep', establishes budget approval, and protects your business from misunderstandings regarding pricing and deliverables.

Unlike a simple price tag, a professional quotation includes: quotation number, validity expiration date (to protect against material cost inflation), itemized project phases, unit costs, estimated delivery timelines, payment terms (such as 50% deposit upfront), and explicit terms and conditions.

FAQ

What is the difference between a price quotation and an invoice?

A quotation is sent before a project begins to propose costs and project scope. An invoice is sent after work is completed (or at agreed milestones) to demand payment.

Why is an expiration or validity date essential on every quotation?

An expiration date (such as 'Valid for 30 days') protects your business from raw material price inflation, vendor rate hikes, or exchange rate fluctuations that occur after you submit your quote.

Can I customize line items, payment terms, and project milestones?

Yes. You can add detailed item descriptions, hourly or fixed rates, advance deposit percentages, and custom terms of service.

Can I include my company branding and logo on the quote?

Yes. Upload your logo and company details to present a polished, corporate proposal that inspires client confidence.

Is my proprietary pricing data stored on your servers?

No. All quotation generation occurs locally in your browser memory. Your client lists and proprietary rates remain strictly confidential.
